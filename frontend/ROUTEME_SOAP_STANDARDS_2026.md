# PART 1: EXACT SOAP STRUCTURE

## 1.1 The Four Sections — Definitive Breakdown

### S — SUBJECTIVE
**What the patient/family/caregiver says.** Verbatim or paraphrased patient-reported information. This section captures the patient's own words, complaints, feelings, and perceptions. No clinical interpretation here — just the patient's story.

**Must include:**
- Chief complaint / reason for visit (in patient's words)
- Pain level (0–10 scale) if applicable
- Symptom description (onset, duration, quality, severity, aggravating/alleviating factors)
- Patient-reported adherence to medications/treatments
- Patient-reported diet, activity, sleep, bowel/bladder function
- Quoted statements where possible (e.g., *Patient states: "I feel short of breath just walking to the bathroom"*)

**Home Health Examples:**

| Visit Type | Subjective Entry |
|---|---|
| Post-op hip replacement | "Patient states: 'My hip is sore this morning but better than yesterday. I took my pain pill about an hour ago.' Rates pain 4/10. Reports eating breakfast and walking to the bathroom with walker." |
| Wound care | "Patient states: 'The wound is draining a little more today. It's red around the edges.' Denies fever or chills. Reports changing dressing as instructed yesterday." |
| Diabetes check | "Patient states: 'My blood sugar was 245 this morning. I haven't been eating exactly right — had some cake at my grandson's birthday.' Reports taking Metformin as prescribed." |
| CHF exacerbation | "Patient reports: 'I'm more short of breath the last two days. My ankles are swollen and I can't sleep lying flat.' States weight was up 4 lbs from yesterday per her daily log." |
| COPD follow-up | "Patient states: 'I ran out of my inhaler three days ago. I'm coughing more and bringing up yellow phlegm.' Reports using neighbor's albuterol inhaler 2x today." |
| Chemo aftercare | "Patient states: 'I'm very tired since the treatment Tuesday. No appetite. Nauseated but no vomiting. Mouth feels dry.' Reports low-grade temp of 99.8°F this morning." |

---

### O — OBJECTIVE
**What the clinician observes, measures, and assesses.** Factual, measurable, observable data. Vital signs, physical exam findings, wound measurements, lab values, functional assessments, and environmental observations. This is the "what you see, hear, measure, and smell" section.

**Must include:**
- Vital signs (BP, HR, RR, temp, O2 sat, pain score)
- Physical assessment findings (organized by body system)
- Wound measurements (length × width × depth, tunneling, undermining, drainage type/amount/odor, periwound condition)
- Functional assessment (gait, transfer ability, fall risk, ADL performance)
- Mental status / cognition
- Medication review (counts, discrepancies, missed doses)
- Environmental assessment (safety hazards, home cleanliness, caregiver availability)
- Diagnostic results (lab values, glucose readings, INR, etc.)
- OASIS items that were assessed (by item number, e.g., M1800, M1860)

**Home Health Examples:**

| Visit Type | Objective Entry |
|---|---|
| Post-op hip | "BP 128/76, HR 78, RR 16, Temp 98.6°F, O2 97% RA, Pain 4/10. Incision: 6" surgical wound, well-approximated edges, slight serosanguinous drainage at distal 1cm, no erythema, no induration. Staples intact × 12. ROM: hip flexion 60°, abduction 20°. Transfers: stand-by assist with walker, steady gait with minimal antalgic lean. Medication count: Oxycodone — 8 of 15 remaining (prescribed 5 days ago)." |
| Wound care | "VSS: BP 132/80, HR 76, RR 14, Temp 97.8°F, O2 98% RA. Stage 3 sacral pressure ulcer: 3.2cm × 2.8cm × 0.5cm depth, 100% red granulation tissue bed, 1cm undermining at 12 o'clock position, moderate serous drainage, no odor. Periwound skin: intact, non-macerated, 1cm surrounding erythema. Wound cultured per orders. Braden Scale: 16." |
| Diabetes | "BP 144/82, HR 88, RR 16, Temp 98.4°F. Fingerstick glucose: 287 mg/dL. Weight: 187 lbs (up 2 lbs from last visit). Foot exam: bilateral pedal pulses +2, sensation intact to monofilament bilaterally, no lesions/macerations. Medication count: Metformin 500mg — 15 of 30 remaining (should be 20 — 5 doses missed). Insulin log reviewed: 12 units AM, 8 units PM — correct per sliding scale." |
| COPD | "BP 138/84, HR 92, RR 22, Temp 99.0°F, O2 91% RA, 94% on 2L NC. Lung sounds: diminished breath sounds bilaterally, scattered expiratory wheezes, prolonged expiratory phase. Cough: productive with moderate thick yellow sputum. Peak flow: 180 L/min (baseline 250). Edema: 1+ bilateral LE. O2 device: portable concentrator set at 2L, tank at 1/4 full. Inhaler technique: poor — patient does not use spacer." |
| Chemo aftercare | "BP 110/68, HR 72, RR 14, Temp 99.8°F, O2 97% RA. Weight: 142 lbs (down 3 lbs from last visit). Oral mucosa: mild erythema, no ulcerations, tongue slightly dry. Skin: no rash, no bruising. Port site: clean, dry, intact, no erythema or tenderness. CBC results from 7/24: WBC 3.2, Hgb 10.1, Plt 145. Patient completing 3rd cycle of FOLFOX." |

---

### A — ASSESSMENT
**Clinical judgment and interpretation.** The nurse's professional analysis connecting the subjective and objective data. This section demonstrates clinical reasoning — what the findings mean, changes from baseline, and clinical impressions.

**Must include:**
- Synthesis of S + O data into a clinical picture
- Comparison to previous visit / baseline / goals
- Problem list with current status per problem
- Response to current treatment plan
- Changes in condition (improved, declined, unchanged)
- Clinical judgment (e.g., "symptoms consistent with worsening CHF")
- Any new problems identified
- OASIS outcome-driven reasoning

**Home Health Examples:**

| Visit Type | Assessment Entry |
|---|---|
| Post-op hip | "Patient is POD 5 s/p right total hip arthroplasty. Incision healing well with no signs of infection. Pain controlled on current regimen (4/10). Mobility improving — progressed from 2-person assist to SBA with walker. Meeting physical therapy goals. Risk for falls remains moderate due to deconditioning and unfamiliarity with hip precautions. No DVT or PE symptoms. Continue current plan." |
| Wound care | "Stage 3 sacral pressure ulcer is showing slow improvement — wound size decreased from 3.5×3.0×0.7cm (last week) to 3.2×2.8×0.5cm. Granulation tissue improved from 75% to 100%. Undermining decreased from 2cm to 1cm. No signs of systemic infection. Offloading compliance is suboptimal — patient tolerating only 30 min of turning schedule. Risk for further skin breakdown on bilateral heels." |
| Diabetes | "Type 2 diabetes poorly controlled — glucose 287 despite Metformin and insulin. Patient reports dietary non-adherence (birthday cake). 5 missed Metformin doses noted. Hemoglobin A1c likely above goal. No evidence of DKA or hyperosmolar state. Foot exam intact. Requires re-education on dietary management and medication adherence. Consider referral to dietitian." |
| COPD | "COPD exacerbation suspected — patient off maintenance inhaler for 3 days, now with increased dyspnea, productive cough with purulent sputum, O2 sat 91% on room air, decreased peak flow. Poor inhaler technique evident. Elevated respiratory rate and use of accessory muscles. At risk for hospitalization if not stabilized. Requires MD notification for medication renewal and possible antibiotic therapy." |
| Cardiac (CHF) | "Worsening CHF exacerbation: 4 lb weight gain in 1 week, increasing SOB, orthopnea, 1+ bilateral LE edema, decreased O2 sat from baseline. Patient likely non-adherent with low-sodium diet (reports eating out). Daily weights not being performed consistently. Requires prompt MD notification for possible diuretic adjustment. High risk for hospital readmission within 30 days." |
| Chemo aftercare | "Post-chemotherapy (Cycle 3, FOLFOX) fatigue and nausea as expected. Mild neutropenia (WBC 3.2) — at increased infection risk. Mild anemia (Hgb 10.1). No active infection signs. Mild dehydration per weight loss, dry mucous membranes, and slightly elevated temp. Oral intake inadequate. Requires aggressive hydration education and antiemetic regimen optimization. Oncology notification warranted." |

---

### P — PLAN
**What the nurse will do next.** The action plan — nursing interventions, coordination, education, referrals, and follow-up schedule. Includes both the current visit actions and the plan until the next visit.

**Must include:**
- Skilled nursing interventions performed
- Treatments rendered (wound care, medication administration, etc.)
- Patient/caregiver education provided
- Orders carried out or requested
- Referrals made (PT, OT, MSW, dietitian, MD)
- Supplies ordered
- Next visit date and frequency
- Specific goals for next visit
- MD/physician notification with details
- 485 Plan of Care updates

**Home Health Examples:**

| Visit Type | Plan Entry |
|---|---|
| Post-op hip | "1. Incision care: wound cleansed with NS, dried, covered with sterile gauze. 2. Reinforced hip precautions (no >90° flexion, no adduction past midline, no internal rotation). 3. Education: signs/symptoms of infection, when to call MD. 4. Encouraged ambulation per PT plan. 5. Next visit: 3 days. 6. MD notified — no changes to orders at this time. 7. Will continue monitoring pain control, incision status, and mobility progress." |
| Wound care | "1. Wound care per orders: wound cleansed with wound cleanser, packed with calcium alginate, covered with foam dressing. 2. Education: signs of wound infection, importance of turning schedule, dietary protein intake for wound healing. 3. Contacted family re: pressure relief schedule compliance. 4. Supplies ordered: foam dressings, calcium alginate, NS, wound cleanser. 5. Next visit: daily × 7 days then reassess. 6. Will notify MD if wound culture returns positive or if wound worsens." |
| Diabetes | "1. Blood glucose monitoring and sliding scale insulin administered per orders. 2. Medication reconciliation completed — educated on importance of daily Metformin compliance. 3. Diabetes re-education: diet management, carbohydrate counting, recognizing hypoglycemia symptoms. 4. Reinforced sick day rules. 5. Referral to dietitian submitted. 6. Next visit: 2 days. 7. Will monitor glucose log, medication count, and dietary compliance. 8. Will notify MD if glucose sustained >250." |
| COPD | "1. Contacted MD — order obtained for azithromycin 250mg daily × 5 days for suspected COPD exacerbation. 2. MD called in new maintenance inhaler Rx to patient's pharmacy. 3. Inhaler education: proper technique with spacer — demonstrated, return demonstration poor, will continue to teach. 4. O2 device: ensured concentrator and portable tank settings at 2L. 5. Encouraged increased fluid intake for mucus clearance. 6. Next visit: 2 days. 7. Will monitor O2 sat, lung sounds, and sputum characteristics. 8. Instructed to call MD or 911 if SOB worsens." |
| Chemo aftercare | "1. Encouraged PO hydration — set goal of 6-8 glasses fluid daily. 2. Antiemetic review: instructed to take ondansetron 8mg TID as scheduled, not PRN only. 3. Nutrition: small frequent meals, protein shakes. 4. Infection prevention education: hand hygiene, avoid crowds, report temp >100.4°F. 5. Mouth care: non-alcohol mouthwash, oral care after meals. 6. Notified oncology of lab results — no orders changes at this time. 7. Next visit: 3 days. 8. Will monitor weight, intake, labs, and symptoms." |

## 1.2 Section Rules Summary

| Aspect | Rule |
|---|---|
| **S — Subjective** | Patient's words only. No clinical interpretation. Use quotation marks for direct quotes. |
| **O — Objective** | Verifiable, measurable data. No opinions. No diagnoses. |
| **A — Assessment** | Nurse's clinical judgment. Must synthesize S+O. Never contradictory to S+O. |
| **P — Plan** | Action-oriented. Must be specific, measurable, and time-bound. Must include next visit date. |
| **Order** | S → O → A → P always. Never reorder. |
| **Tenses** | S in present tense ("Patient states..."), O in past tense ("BP was 128/76"), A in present tense ("Patient is at risk for..."), P in future tense ("Will monitor..."). |
| **Length** | No minimum/maximum. Complete enough to tell the full story. |
| **Brevity** | Use approved abbreviations (see Joint Commission "Do Not Use" list). |

# PART 2: LEGAL REQUIREMENTS & DEFENSIBILITY

## 2.1 What Makes a SOAP Note Legally Defensible

A legally defensible SOAP note is one that would stand up in court, during an audit, or in a licensing board review. The standard is: **"If it wasn't documented, it wasn't done."**

### Essential Elements

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Date and Time** | Every entry must have a date AND a specific time (24-hour or HH:MM AM/PM). Include date of service AND date of entry. |
| **Author Identification** | Full name, title/credentials (RN, LPN, PT, OT, MSW), and preferably NPI number. Must be legible (printed or typed). |
| **Signature** | Electronic signature (EHR) or handwritten. In EHR, must include authentication method (e.g., "Electronically signed by Jane Doe, RN on 01/15/2026 at 14:30"). |
| **Timeliness** | Ideally entered immediately after visit. Generally acceptable within 24–48 hours for home health. Anything beyond that requires a late entry policy. |
| **Contemporaneous** | Entry should reflect the visit as it happened, not reconstructed. |
| **Factual, Not Opinion** | "Patient appeared intoxicated" = opinion. "Patient speech was slurred, gait was unsteady, breath smelled of alcohol" = fact. |
| **Complete** | Must tell a complete story — what happened, what was done, what was taught, what was planned. No gaps that could be interpreted as omissions. |
| **Legible** | If handwritten, must be readable by someone other than the writer. |
| **No Alterations** | No erasures, white-out, or deletions. Corrections must follow a specific policy. |
| **Non-Contributory** | No blaming, no assumptions, no judgmental language. Document objectively. |

### The "4 Cs" of Documentation

| C | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Clear** | Unambiguous, specific language. Avoid vague terms like "good," "fair," "normal" without quantification. |
| **Concise** | Brevity without sacrificing completeness. |
| **Complete** | All relevant information present. No gaps in the clinical picture. |
| **Chronological** | Entries in order by date/time. No back-dating without proper late entry protocol. |

### Common Legal Pitfalls in Home Health SOAP Notes

| Pitfall | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Copy-forward without updating | Same assessment for 3 visits in a row | Always update to reflect current status |
| Vague language | "Patient doing well" | "Patient ambulating 50 ft with walker, pain 2/10, incision healing without complication" |
| No response to abnormal findings | "BP 180/100" with no plan or MD notification | Document the notification and response |
| Charting care that wasn't given | Charting a dressing change that didn't happen | Document exactly what was done |
| Late entries without proper format | Adding a note 5 days later with no explanation | Follow late entry policy |
| Using opinion as fact | "Patient is non-compliant" | "Patient has missed 5 of 30 Metformin doses. Patient reports choosing not to take medication on weekends." |
| Gaps in narrative | 3 days between visits but no phone contact or note | Document any phone calls or attempts to reach patient |

## 2.2 Timestamps and Signatures

### EHR Timestamp Requirements

| Element | Standard |
|---|---|
| **Date of Service** | Must match the actual visit date |
| **Time of Service** | Visit start and end time (e.g., 09:30 – 10:45) |
| **Time of Entry** | When the note was actually written/entered (may differ from service time) |
| **Time of Signature** | When the note was authenticated/signed |
| **Audit Trail** | EHR must capture every view, edit, and re-sign with timestamp and user ID |

### Signature Requirements

| Type | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Electronic** | Must be unique to the user. Must use a secure authentication method (password, biometric, or token). CMS requires EHR signatures to meet the e-signature standard per 21 CFR Part 11. |
| **Handwritten** | Must include first initial, last name, and credentials. Must be legible. |
| **Stamped** | Not acceptable unless accompanied by a handwritten signature over the stamp. |
| **Verbal Orders** | Must be signed within 30 days by the physician per CMS Conditions of Participation. |

## 2.3 Late Entry Policy

### CMS/HIPAA Standards for Late Entries

A late entry is any entry made after the standard documentation timeframe (usually 24–48 hours for home health).

**Late Entry Format:**

```
[Current Date & Time]
LATE ENTRY — [Date of Service]
Reason for late entry: [Brief explanation — e.g., "system outage," "patient
visit extended beyond documentation window," "additional information received"]
[Body of entry — same S-O-A-P format]

signed: [Name, Credentials] on [Date/Time]
```

**Rules:**
- Never insert a late entry directly into the original visit note — it must be a separate addendum
- Always label it clearly as "LATE ENTRY"
- State the reason (briefly and factually)
- Include the original date of service
- Date and sign with the current date
- Do not alter the original note
- Late entries for Medicare/Medicaid billing must comply with CMS Chapter 3 §30.2.1

## 2.4 Correction/Amendment Policy (HIPAA & Joint Commission)

### HIPAA Right to Amend (45 CFR § 164.526)

Under HIPAA, patients have the right to request an amendment to their health information. The provider must act on the request within 60 days (extendable by 30 days with written notice).

### Correction Format (Clinician-Initiated)

When a clinician discovers an error in their own documentation:

**For EHR Systems:**
1. **Do not delete** the original entry — the system must preserve the audit trail
2. **Create an addendum** or correction note
3. Clearly identify the incorrect entry being corrected
4. State the correction
5. Provide the reason for the correction (do not use blame language)
6. Date and sign the correction

**Standard Correction Format:**

```
ADDENDUM/CORRECTION to note dated [Date of original note] at [Time]
Entry: [Briefly identify what was incorrect]
Correction: [State the correct information]
Reason: [Factual reason — e.g., "transcription error," "value corrected after
lab results verified"]
[signed: Name, Credentials, Date/Time]
```

**Joint Commission Standard (RC.01.03.01):**
- The original content must remain legible and readable
- The correction must be clearly identified as a correction
- The correction must be dated and timed
- The person making the correction must be identified
- In EHR, the audit trail must show the original content, the corrected content, and the date/time of both

### Prohibited Correction Methods

| Method | Why It's Prohibited |
|---|---|
| White-out / correction fluid | Alters the record; destroys original content |
| Erasing | Destroys original content — fraudulent |
| Deleting EHR entries | Violates audit trail requirements |
| Overwriting | Same problem — destroys original |
| Removing pages | Record tampering |
| Back-dating | Fraudulent misrepresentation of timeline |

### Patient-Requested Amendment Process

1. Patient submits written request for amendment (identifying the specific record)
2. Provider reviews within 60 days
3. If accepted: provider amends the record and notifies all parties who received the original
4. If denied: provider must provide written denial with reason, and patient can submit a statement of disagreement
5. The original record is never deleted — the amendment is an addendum

## 2.5 Home Health vs. Hospital SOAP Notes — Key Differences

| Aspect | Hospital | Home Health |
|---|---|---|
| **Setting context** | Assume hospital environment | Document home environment, safety, caregiver availability |
| **Frequency** | Every shift (q8-12h) | Each visit (may be daily, weekly, etc.) |
| **OASIS integration** | Not required | OASIS items must be assessed and documented at specified timepoints |
| **Functional focus** | Acute care needs | ADLs, IADLs, home safety, caregiver support |
| **Medication reconciliation** | Per shift | Each visit — count pills, verify against 485 orders |
| **Caregiver involvement** | Minimal documentation | Essential — document caregiver presence, capability, training |
| **Home environment** | Not relevant | Critical — document safety hazards, home conditions, access issues |
| **485 Plan of Care** | N/A | Must reference the 485 in every note |
| **Physician notification** | Via hospital system | Nurse must document direct communication with MD |
| **Supplies/equipment** | Provided by facility | Document equipment status, supply needs, DME coordination |
| **Skilled need justification** | Implicit in admission | Must explicitly justify continued skilled nursing need |
| **Billing tie-in** | DRG-based | Visit-based — documentation must support visit medical necessity |
| **Telehealth** | Rare | Growing — document virtual visit components separately |

## 2.6 OASIS Item Mapping in SOAP Notes

The Outcome and Assessment Information Set (OASIS-E, effective January 2023, with 2024–2026 updates) drives home health reimbursement. SOAP notes should cross-reference OASIS items.

### Key OASIS Items to Reference in SOAP Notes

| OASIS Item | Description | Where to Document in SOAP |
|---|---|---|
| **M1800** | Grooming | O — observe patient grooming ability |
| **M1810** | Current ability to dress upper body | O — functional assessment |
| **M1820** | Current ability to dress lower body | O — functional assessment |
| **M1830** | Bathing | O — observe bathing safety/ability |
| **M1840** | Toilet transferring | O — observe toileting independence |
| **M1850** | Transferring | O — observe transfer technique |
| **M1860** | Ambulation/Locomotion | O — gait assessment, distance ambulated |
| **M1910** | Falls risk | A — fall risk assessment |
| **M2020** | Management of oral medications | O — medication count, pillbox check |
| **M2030** | Injectable medication management | O — insulin administration assessment |
| **M2102** | Caregiver availability/ability | O — caregiver assessment |
| **M2200** | Therapy need | P — therapy referrals |
| **M2300** | Emergent care | O — if ED visit occurred |
| **M2401** | Intervention synopsis | A — summary of interventions |
| **M1021/1023** | ICD-10 diagnosis codes | A/P — problem list, plan linkage |

**SOAP Note Pattern for OASIS Integration:**
```
O — M1860: Patient ambulated 75 ft with front-wheeled walker, minimal
    assist, rest break needed at 50 ft. M1850: Transfer from bed to w/c
    with stand-by assist, steady.
A — Functional status declining per M1860 compared to start of care.
    Requires continued PT for gait training and strengthening.
P — Will reassess M1860/M1850 at next visit. Continue PT 3x/week.
```

# PART 3: ICD-10 CODING PLACEMENT

## 3.1 Where ICD-10 Codes Go in SOAP Notes

ICD-10 codes are typically placed in the **Assessment (A)** section, with a clear linkage to the clinical problems identified. Some formats also include a separate "Diagnosis" line between A and P.

### Recommended Placement

**Option A — Within Assessment section (preferred):**
```
A — Assessment:
1. [ICD-10 Code] — Diagnosis description — Clinical status
2. [ICD-10 Code] — Diagnosis description — Clinical status
```

**Option B — Separate Diagnosis Line (also acceptable):**
```
A — Assessment: [Clinical narrative]

Diagnoses:
1. E11.9 — Type 2 diabetes mellitus without complications — poorly controlled
2. L97.921 — Non-pressure chronic ulcer of left calf with fat layer exposed — improving
3. I10 — Essential hypertension — stable

P — Plan: ...
```

### Coding Format Rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Code specificity** | Always use the most specific code available (e.g., E11.42, not E11.9) |
| **Primary diagnosis** | Code that most closely relates to the skilled need for home health |
| **Secondary diagnoses** | All active, managed conditions during the episode |
| **Code order** | Follow the 485 Plan of Care order (or acuity order) |
| **Z-codes** | Use Z-codes for social determinants, aftercare, and encounters (e.g., Z48.815 for aftercare of hip replacement, Z51.11 for chemotherapy) |
| **External cause codes** | Required for injuries (e.g., W19.XXXA for fall) |
| **Manifestation codes** | When applicable, use both etiology and manifestation (e.g., E11.42 + H36.0 for diabetic retinopathy) |
| **Code updates** | ICD-10 codes update annually on October 1. Must use current year's codes. |

# PART 4: COMMON CONDITION TEMPLATES

## 4.1 Post-Operative Care (e.g., Hip Replacement)

"Patient states: 'Pain is [x/10] at the incision site. [Better/worse/same] compared to yesterday. I [did/did not] take my pain medication [time].' Reports [ability to ambulate/transfer/appetite/bowel movement]. Denies [fever/chills/nausea/dizziness/chest pain/SOB]."

**O:**
- Vitals: [BP/HR/RR/Temp/O2/Pain]
- Incision: [length] [cm/in], [staples/sutures/steri-strips/clips] × [count], [well-approximated/gaping], [drainage type/amount], [periwound condition]
- Neurovascular status: [CMS distal to surgical site — color, motion, sensation, capillary refill]
- ROM: [hip/knee flexion/extension] [degrees]
- Gait: [distance] with [assistive device] [level of assist]
- Transfers: [independent/SBA/min/mod assist]
- Edema: [presence/location/grade]
- DVT prevention: [compression devices/medication/compliance]
- Bowel function: [last BM, regularity]
- Pain mgmt: [medication count, effectiveness]

"Patient is [POD #] s/p [surgery type]. Incision healing [as expected/with concerns — specify]. Pain [controlled/poorly controlled]. Functional status [improving/stable/declining] from last visit. [Risk for falls/DVT/infection/wound dehiscence]. [meeting/not meeting] physical therapy goals."

**P:**
- [ ] Wound care per orders
- [ ] Reinforced [precautions/restrictions]
- [ ] Education: [S/S of infection, DVT, wound care, activity restrictions]
- [ ] Medication management: [pain control review, adherence check]
- [ ] Next visit: [date]
- [ ] MD notified re: [reason and response]

## 4.2 Wound Care

**S:**
"Patient states: 'The wound [feels better/worse/same].' Reports drainage [amount/color] on dressing. [Denies/endorses] pain at wound site [x/10]. [Denies/endorses] fever, chills, redness spreading. Reports [compliance/non-compliance] with turning/pressure relief schedule."

**O:**
- Vitals: [BP/HR/RR/Temp/O2]
- Wound Assessment:
  - Location: [anatomical site]
  - Stage: [1/2/3/4/Unstageable/Deep Tissue Injury]
  - Size: L × W × D [cm]
  - Wound bed: [% granulation / % slough / % eschar / % epithelialization]
  - Exudate: [type: serous/sanguinous/seropurulent/purulent] [amount: none/scant/small/mod/large]
  - Odor: [yes/no — describe]
  - Tunneling/Undermining: [location, depth in cm]
  - Periwound: [intact/macerated/erythematous/indurated/excoriated]
  - Edges: [open/rolled/attached/undermined]
- Pain at wound: [x/10]
- Braden Scale score: [total and subscale breakdown]
- Nutritional status: [albumin, prealbumin, dietary intake]
- Offloading: [pressure relief device compliance]

**A:**
"Wound [improving/unchanged/worsening] compared to [last measurement date]. Current treatment [appropriate/needs revision]. [Infection risk/actual infection present with/without systemic signs]. [Nutritional/offloading concerns]. Healing trajectory [on track/delayed] for wound type and patient factors."

**P:**
- [ ] Wound care per orders: [cleanser/agent/dressing change]
- [ ] Wound photographed [if applicable]
- [ ] Culture obtained [if ordered]
- [ ] Education: [S/S infection, turning schedule, protein intake, dressing change technique]
- [ ] Supplies ordered: [list]
- [ ] Nutrition: [protein supplement encouraged, dietitian referral if needed]
- [ ] Next visit: [date]
- [ ] MD notification: [if wound deteriorating, culture results, or orders needed]

## 4.3 Diabetes Management

**S:**
"Patient reports blood glucose [value] at [time] today. [List meals/snacks consumed]. 'I felt [shaky/sweaty/dizzy/tired/thirsty]' [when]. Reports [taking/missing] insulin/oral medication as prescribed. [Denies/endorses] hypoglycemic episodes. [Denies/endorses] signs of hyperglycemia (polyuria, polydipsia, nocturia). Reports foot check [daily/occasionally/never]."

# PART 6: 2025–2026 UPDATES & CMS CHANGES

## 6.1 Key CMS Regulatory Updates for Home Health (2024–2026)

### Home Health Prospective Payment System (HH PPS) — 2025 Final Rule

- **Rate Adjustment:** 2.5% increase in home health payments for CY 2025 (effective January 1, 2025)
- **LUPA Thresholds:** Updated Low Utilization Payment Adjustment thresholds based on updated data
- **NLU (Non-Routine Supplies):** Continued phase of NRS conversion to case-mix adjusted model
- **OASIS-E Continued:** OASIS-E remains the required assessment instrument (no OASIS-F announced yet for 2026)
- **Value-Based Purchasing (HHVBP):** Expanded to all 50 states — documentation quality directly impacts payment adjustments

### 2026 Proposed/Expected Changes

| Potentially Effective | Description |
|---|---|
| **January 2026** | HH PPS CY 2026 Final Rule — expected rate update (likely 1.5–3.5% based on market basket) |
| **OASIS-E Refinements** | No new OASIS version expected for 2026; CMS continues to refine OASIS-E guidance per the 2023–2025 implementation feedback |
| **PDGM Stability** | Patient-Driven Groupings Model remains the payment model — no major redesign expected |
| **Telehealth Flexibilities** | Ongoing monitoring — CMS has maintained certain telehealth flexibilities for home health, but in-person visits remain the standard for most skilled services |
| **Social Determinants of Health (SDOH)** | Continued emphasis on Z-code documentation and SDOH screening in home health assessments |
| **Health Equity** | CMS continues to require health equity data collection across home health agencies |

### Documentation-Specific CMS Updates

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **85/15 Rule** | Per the 2025 HH PPS Final Rule, CMS clarified that home health agencies must ensure at least 85% of visits are in-person (only 15% may be telehealth) |
| **Face-to-Face Encounter** | Must occur within 90 days before or 30 days after start of care. Documentation must include the certifying physician's statement confirming the encounter. |
| **Plan of Care (485)** | Must be reviewed and signed by the physician every 60 days. The SOAP note must demonstrate that the care provided aligns with the 485. |
| **Medical Necessity** | CMS continues to audit for medical necessity — each SOAP note must justify the need for skilled nursing. "Patient stable" alone does not justify continued care. Must show what skilled intervention was provided. |
| **Timely Documentation** | CMS requires documentation to be completed within 30 days of the visit, but individual agencies and accreditation bodies may require shorter windows (typically 24–48 hours). |
| **OASIS Collection Timepoints** | SOC/ROC (start/resumption), follow-up (every 60 days), transfer, discharge, and death. SOAP notes must align with OASIS collection timepoints. |

## 6.3 Best Practices for 2026

### Smart SOAP Note Writing

1. **Patient-Centered Language:** Use "Patient reports" rather than "Patient claims" or "Patient states they didn't" (avoids judgmental tone)
2. **Quantify Everything:** Not "wound looks better" but "wound size decreased from 4.2×3.1×0.8cm to 3.8×2.9×0.6cm, granulation increased from 60% to 85%"
3. **Link to Goals:** Every note should reference the patient's specific goals from the 485 Plan of Care
4. **Show Skilled Need:** Explicitly state what ONLY a registered nurse could do (e.g., assessment, wound care, medication management, teaching)
5. **Document What Didn't Work:** If the patient refused treatment or education, document it factually — this protects the nurse and the agency
6. **Use Standardized Scales:** Braden, Morse Fall Scale, PHQ-2/9, pain scales, dyspnea scales — use validated tools consistently
7. **Close the Loop:** Every abnormal finding should have a documented response (MD notification, intervention, follow-up plan)
8. **Avoid Copy-Forward Pitfalls:** If using EHR templates, always verify that all data has been updated for the current visit

### Common Documentation Deficiencies Cited by CMS

| Deficiency | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Missing or incomplete OASIS items | Highest |
| Lack of medical necessity justification | High |
| Generic/unchanged assessments across visits | High |
| Missing physician notification documentation | Moderate |
| Incomplete medication reconciliation | Moderate |
| Late entries without proper documentation | Moderate |
| Missing or illegible signatures | Low (EHRs reduce this) |
| Inconsistent data (OASIS contradicts visit note) | Moderate |
